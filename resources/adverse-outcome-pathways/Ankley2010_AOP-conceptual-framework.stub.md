## Abstract (paraphrased from verified PubMed record)
This is the seminal paper that introduced the Adverse Outcome Pathway (AOP) concept. An AOP is a conceptual construct that portrays existing knowledge concerning the linkage between a direct **molecular initiating event (MIE)** and an **adverse outcome (AO)** at a biological level of organization relevant to risk assessment. The framework organizes toxicological knowledge as causal linkages between an MIE and subsequent measurable responses (**key events, KEs**) across biological levels of organization, culminating in an adverse outcome of regulatory significance. The authors argue this approach can improve species selection and extrapolation, enhance chemical comparison and category formation, support forecasting of mixture effects, and enable use of molecular/biochemical biomarkers to predict impacts at the organism and population levels.

## Relevance to PhysioMap
The origin of the cross-scale, signed causal-pathway abstraction PhysioMap formalizes. KE = a (biological entity, quality/state-change) node typed by biological scale; KER = a directed causal edge; MIE/AO = scale-anchored boundary nodes. Establishes the principle that biology can be encoded as a chain of measurable state changes linked by causal relationships across levels of organization — exactly PhysioMap's node/edge ontology, minus the explicit feedback cycles (AOPs were originally acyclic).
